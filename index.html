Hi, I'm Sumera 👋

I'm a PhD student, a serial learner-of-new-things, and a person who genuinely wants to know why an AI system said what it said. This page is a little about my research, and a little about the human behind it.

🔬What I'm Working On

I'm pursuing my PhD with Dr. Yunhe Feng in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at the University of North Texas, as part of the Responsible AI Lab. The question that drives me is simple to ask and hard to answer: how do we make large language models genuinely worth trusting? That takes me through 🛡️ trustworthy LLMs, 🤖 agentic AI and the 🔌 Model Context Protocol (MCP), and ⚛️ generative AI for quantum computing.

In practice, I chase that question from three directions: catching and reducing hallucinations where the stakes are highest — clinical decision support (HALO, ACM/IEEE CHASE 2025); opening up the black box of agent systems to profile the token flows and latency costs hiding inside MCP-based agents (ProMCP, ACL Findings 2026, oral); and teaching LLMs to write quantum programs across PennyLane, Qiskit, and OpenQASM — then grading them not on how the code looks, but on whether it actually runs.

🧭The Road Here

My path runs from Hyderabad to Texas. I did my B.Tech in Computer Science at Sreenidhi Institute of Science and Technology (2019–2023, GPA 8.91/10), then spent a semester at 📦 Amazon (AWS) on the Kindle Reader team, where I built a self-service diagnostic tool on Lambda, S3, and SNS that automated issue detection and root-cause analysis — my first taste of building things people actually depend on. I came to UNT for my 💻 MS in Computer Science (2023–2025), finished with a 🏆 4.0 GPA and the Distinguished Graduate Student Award, and loved the research too much to stop. I've been a Graduate Research Assistant in the Responsible AI Lab since 2023 and a Teaching Assistant since 2024.

🎨When I Close the Laptop

If there is one thread running through my life, it is a stubborn, joyful appetite for learning things from scratch. I speak 🗣️ Hindi, Urdu, Telugu, and English, and I'm currently adding Chinese and Arabic to the collection — because apparently studying how language models work all day wasn't enough languages for one person.

Away from the terminal, I pick up a brush instead of a keyboard: 🎨 painting and drawing intricate 🌿 henna (mehndi) designs are my favorite ways to think with my hands. I believe in collecting skills that make me more capable as a person, not just as a researcher — so this past year I learned to 🚗 drive and to 🏊 swim, and my current project is 🍳 cooking, which I'm learning one slightly-burnt experiment at a time.

I love ✈️ traveling and collecting new places the way I collect new languages. And after all of that, I am a fierce defender of my most underrated hobby: 😴 sleep, which I consider essential infrastructure for everything above.
